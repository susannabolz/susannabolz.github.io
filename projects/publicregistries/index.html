Public registries

Public registries are a powerful source of research data. I will give you a short overview of some of the registries I use in my research.


Marktstammdatenregister

I use the Marktstammdatenregister data to investigate whether the foundation of local energy agencies accelerates the expansion of solar PV in Germany (Bolz, 2026). I published R code to process available bulk downloads here. In case you mainly work with Python, check out this package.


EU-ETS

Jan Abrell prepared and published EU-ETS transaction data to make them easily accessible for investigating the effects of a cap-and-trade policy. I use these EU-ETS data to measure regulatory pressure and investigate whether it affects how sustainability reports are written (Birkholz et al., 2026).


German and European funding data

CORDIS provides access to European research projects funded under FP7, Horizon, and Horizon 2020.

The German Förderkatalog is a database that covers projects funded by federal ministries. You can download the data as csv.

2026

  1. Do Local Energy Agencies Accelerate the Solar Expansion in Germany? [Under review at Environmental Innovation and Sustainability Transitions]
    Susanna Bolz
    2026
    Abs

    While national policies are essential, local authorities have agency to implement additional measures to accelerate the transition. This paper examines the impact of a specific measure at the county level, \acEAs, on solar PV adoption in Germany. These agencies, founded since 1992, serve as contact points for citizens, municipalities, and companies, focusing on consultancy and networking for renewable energy and energy efficiency. Primarily using data from the Core Energy Market Data Registry and an originally compiled dataset on \acEA, the analysis utilizes augmented synthetic controls to account for staggered agency adoption. Overall, results indicate that both geographical proximity and institutional settings matter for the effectiveness of \acEAs. While effect sizes in general are small and the standard errors are large, a stable pattern emerges: The foundation of a \acEA increases the number of new actors that install rooftop solar PV for the first time, but only to a limited extent. The effect is higher for \acEAs with a solar focus, in counties where there is an LEAs office, and in smaller counties.

  2. Industry Competition, Regulation, and Climate Action: Evidence from the German Sustainability Code Disclosures [Unpublished Manuscript]
    Jessica Birkholz, Susanna Bolz, and Wiebke Siebert
    2026
    Abs

    This paper studies how competitive and regulatory pressures shape firms’ sustainability reporting behavior. Using sustainability reports published under the German Sustainability Code between 2010 and 2024, we examine how industry competition and regulatory exposure affect the quantity and quality of climate-related disclosures. Climate-related commitments and actions are extracted from 3,760 reports using ClimateBERT-based text analysis, enabling the construction of novel sustainability-reporting indicators. Competitive pressure is measured using industry-level markups derived from firm-level accounting data, while regulatory pressure is proxied by exposure to the EU Emissions Trading System. Our results suggests that stronger competition tends to be associated with a higher volume of climate-related discussion and commitments, but with less specificity in these commitments. In contrast, regulatory pressure increases the number and specificity of reported commitments without affecting the overall volume of climate-related discussion. These findings suggest that sustainability reporting may reflects firms’ strategic responses to both market competition and environmental regulation.
